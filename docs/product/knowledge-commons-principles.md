# Knowledge commons principles

Knowledge is living. Different interpretations can coexist. VDanceHub documents, connects,
refines, and credits them without forcing every community to agree.

## Three connected forms

1. **Open discussions** let dancers ask questions, share videos and teaching experiences, compare
   terminology, disagree respectfully, and explore without needing a final conclusion.
2. **Living pages** organize what the community is learning. They retain revision history,
   contributor credit, sources, aliases, and links back to the conversations that shaped them.
3. **Reviewed editions** are immutable, carefully presented snapshots with a stated audience and
   scope. Review means clarity, responsible attribution, completeness, and usefulness—not universal
   correctness.

A reviewed edition never closes its living page or discussion. Another group may publish a later
edition or an edition with a different purpose, teaching system, regional perspective, or historical
interpretation. Older editions remain accessible.

## Pluralism with evidence

Different teaching preferences, names, regional variations, and curriculum sequences can coexist
with context about whom they serve. Contradictory factual or historical claims should be presented
with their evidence and uncertainty rather than flattened into equally established facts.

Curricula may reuse shared pages while retaining their own terminology, order, explanations, and
identity. No curriculum owns a shared movement page, and a shared page does not impose one universal
learning sequence.

## Stewardship

Knowledge stewards improve structure, presentation, attribution, and usefulness. Moderators maintain
safety. Neither role makes someone the supreme authority on dance technique. The software should
preserve revisions, sources, and relationships without forcing everyday contributors to understand
the underlying data model.
